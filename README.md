# Ivy Cottage

A calm little browser game: a forgotten cottage needs some love. Mow the lawn,
scrub the path, pull weeds, paint the fence, the door and the walls in your own
colours, wash the windows, skim the pond, scrub moss off the roof and dust the
attic. Every finished job restores a piece of the cottage and earns coins;
coins buy better tools, little helper robots, decorations and new places:
a little corner café to bring back to life with some light building and
planting, a seaside cove with a beach hut, a rowing boat, an old camper
van and a lighthouse, an animal rescue on an old farm, where dogs need
feeding and walking, a cat wants brushing and the rabbits fresh straw, a
walled garden with an old glasshouse, a canal lock with a narrowboat, an
old bookshop with a reading nook, a pottery with a kiln and a potter's wheel,
a little country station where no train has stopped for years, a tiny shop
of buttons and beads, an old curiosity shop with a box nobody has opened
in years, a little pony yard, a painter's studio in an old hay loft, a small
observatory on a hill with a copper dome, and an art deco lido whose pool is
drained in winter and filled for the summer. New places open over the in-game
years, not all at once. Some jobs take a little thinking
instead of a tool, and some are made with your own hands; a puzzle room, open
from the start, is where new ones are tried out first.

No timers, no losing, no ads, no accounts. Works offline once opened.

Play: https://peterrahbek.github.io/ivy-cottage/

## How it plays

- **Jobs.** Drag a finger over the mess. The tool sits just above your finger
  so you can see what you are doing. Coins fly to the counter as you clean.
  You finish a job yourself: past 90% the leftover spots twinkle (faster the
  fewer there are), and only the last half percent of specks is swept up for
  you, or the last 1.5% once you have hunted for it for five seconds.
- **Tools.** Each kind of job has its own tool line (shears to garden tractor,
  scrubbing brush to turbo washer, small brush to paint sprayer, dishcloth to
  steam cleaner for the coffee machine and the café tables, bath sponge to
  bubble shower for the dog, a coffee pot to a barista trolley for pouring,
  a scoop to a feed trolley for feeding, one dog on a lead to a walking belt
  with five, a soft brush to a double brush for the cat, a hand fork to a
  wheelbarrow for mucking out ...). Every place has its own toolbox: tools
  stay where you buy them, a new place starts with simple tools, and tools
  cost twice as much at the café, three times as much at the cove, four
  times as much at the rescue and five, six, seven, eight and nine times as
  much at the glasshouse, the canal, the bookshop, the pottery and the
  station, and ten times as much from the bead shop on. The next
  tool can be bought mid-job from the button at the bottom, or in the shed.
  Tap the tool at the bottom to switch to any tool you already own (the shears
  are sometimes nicer than the tractor).
- **Robots.** Tidy-bots from the shed help on every job; the robot button at
  the top sends them home (or calls them back) in the middle of a job.
- **Painting.** Picking another colour mid-job repaints what is already done
  as well, so the door ends up the colour you chose.
- **Finds.** Every job hides a keepsake under the mess (a ladybird, an old
  brass key, a friendly frog ...) plus a few coin stashes. The Finds shelf
  collects them, folded by place: the place you are at is open, every other
  place is one line with its count ("12 of 15"), and a tap opens or folds it.
  The seasons' own secrets fold together as one more line.
- **The cottage.** 20 jobs restore the whole house. Painted colours carry over
  to the cottage picture.
- **How new places open.** A place goes up for sale when three things are
  true, ticked off one by one on its card in Places: the place before it is
  restored, enough seasons have turned since then (none for the café, one for
  the cove, two for the rescue, three for the glasshouse, a whole year for the
  canal, the bookshop, the pottery, the station, the bead shop, the curiosity
  shop, the stables, the loft, the observatory and the lido), and there are
  enough coins. Seasons only turn when
  their jobs are done, never on their own, so the wait is always in your own
  hands and nothing is lost while waiting. The card says which season the
  place opens in and how many seasons are left; the season that opens it says
  so once, quietly. A game from before this rule keeps every place it could
  already reach; the canal, the bookshop, the pottery, the station, the bead
  shop, the curiosity shop, the stables, the loft, the observatory and the lido
  wait from then on.
- **Places.** Once the cottage is restored, Marigold Café is for sale (9,000
  coins). Its 16 jobs: sweep the floor, wash the shop window, scrub the
  terrace, wipe the tables, polish the coffee machine, clean the awning, paint
  the sign and the door, and new kinds of job: lay a deck, rebuild a brick
  wall, tile behind the counter (pieces click into place as you work), plant
  planter boxes, a herb box and flower pots (plants spring up), stock the
  drinks fridge, and a puzzle: put the cups away.
- **Seashell Cove.** Once the café is restored the cove is for sale (20,000
  coins). Its 18 jobs include picking up litter and collecting shells (things
  fly into a bin or a bucket), raking seaweed, a sandcastle, planting the
  dunes, a boardwalk and a jetty, the beach hut, the boat, the rock pool, the
  lighthouse, and a camper van in four steps: vacuum, wash, wax, polish.
- **Bluebell Rescue.** Once the cove is restored the rescue is for sale
  (30,000 coins). Its 19 jobs: breakfast and fresh water for the dogs (fill
  every bowl), walk the dogs along a path (paw prints follow you), muck out
  the rabbit hutch, bath a muddy puppy, tidy the dog toys into their box, brush
  a fluffy cat, feed the seal pup, the duck pond, the paddock fence and grass,
  new boards in the barn, carrots for the rabbits, and the farmhouse door,
  office window and sign, plus two puzzles: feeding time and who sleeps
  where. The animals never need you: nothing goes hungry or sad while you are
  away.
- **Primrose Glasshouse.** Once the rescue is restored the walled garden is
  for sale (42,000 coins). Its 15 jobs: the brick path, the grimy glasshouse,
  the long border, the orchard grass, raised beds to build and sow, windfall
  apples into a basket, the potting shed and its roof, the old garden wall,
  the lily pond, pots by the door, the door in the wall, and two puzzles:
  water the glasshouse and the cutting garden (one of each flower in every row
  and column).
- **Kingfisher Lock.** A year after the glasshouse is restored the canal is
  for sale (55,000 coins). Its 15 jobs: moss on the towpath, duckweed on the
  canal, litter along the towpath, the long grass on the bank, the grimy
  portholes, paint the narrowboat (roses and castles come with the fresh
  paint), mend the lock gates and the humpback bridge, pots on the boat roof,
  the lock-keeper's fence, door and window, the little rowing boat, the lock
  sign, and a puzzle: load the boat.
- **Hollyhock Books.** A year after the canal is restored the bookshop is for
  sale (70,000 coins). Its 15 jobs: the shop floor, the shop window, the
  striped awning, the coffee corner, the sign and the door, window boxes, the
  shop cat, tea for the readers, the reading tables, the dusty stockroom, the
  courtyard, the hollyhock bed, a new reading bench, and a puzzle: back on
  the shelves.
- **Kiln Lane Pottery.** A year after the bookshop is restored the pottery is
  for sale (80,000 coins). Its 15 jobs: the cobbled yard, the workshop window
  and floor, soot on the kiln and moss on its chimney, the clay-caked wheel,
  the dusty glaze shelves, the barn door and the sign, a new drying rack,
  clay scraps into the bin, lavender by the kiln, and three jobs done by
  hand: throw a pot on the wheel (tap the pedal, pull the clay up three
  times, dip it in the glaze the order asks for), glaze the tea set (each pot
  to its own shelf and glaze) and load the kiln (every pot onto the kiln's
  shelves).
- **Halfpenny Halt.** A year after the pottery is restored the station is for
  sale (90,000 coins). Its 15 jobs: moss on the platform, the waiting-room
  floor and window, the signal-box windows, the station clock, the platform
  lamps, the station sign, the ticket-office door, the crossing gate, a new
  platform bench, hanging baskets, litter along the line, and three little
  mysteries: lost property (read the notes on the wall and give everything
  back to its owner), stamps for the parcels (stamps that add up to each
  parcel's weight) and what's missing from the shelves (find the five things
  on the list).
- **Button & Bead.** A year after the station is restored the bead shop is
  for sale (100,000 coins). Its 15 jobs: the front step, the bow window, the
  striped awning, the sign and the door, threads on the floor, the brass
  scales, the sewing machine, the dusty button drawers, shelves for the
  ribbons, window boxes, the back yard, and three jobs made by hand: string
  the beads (carry the pattern on, bead by bead), sort the buttons (each
  button to the jar with its colour and its number of holes) and a bracelet
  for the window.
- **Marchbank's Curiosities.** A year after the bead shop is restored the
  curiosity shop is for sale (110,000 coins). Its 15 jobs: the bow-fronted
  window, the sign, the green door, the creaky floor, the cabinet of drawers,
  the grandfather clock, the dusty globe, the brass lamp, the stone fountain,
  the mossy yard, new shelves, ferns in the yard, and three little mysteries:
  the locked box (count what the clues show and turn the dials until the lid
  opens), the jigsaw on the table and things lost among the curiosities.
- **Foxglove Stables.** A year after the curiosity shop is restored the stables
  are for sale (120,000 coins). Its 15 jobs: the muddy yard, the paddock grass,
  the paddock fence, the stable doors, the jumps, the yard sign, the tack-room
  window, the dusty tack room, mucking out the stable, grooming the pony, the
  water trough, new boards on the hay barn, apples for the ponies, ribbons in
  the mane (a pattern to carry on, by hand) and whose saddle is whose (notes on
  the board). In the seasons you can also lead the pony round the paddock. The
  ponies and the yard dog are looked after already and never need anything.
- **The Painter's Loft.** A year after the stables are restored the loft is for
  sale (130,000 coins). Its 15 jobs: the skylight, the loft window, paint on the
  floor, the brushes, the store of frames, the door, the sign, the swing seat,
  the outside stairs, the yard, poppies by the wall, window boxes, and three
  painted by hand: a canvas and the mural wall (paint by numbers, and they stay
  in the place's picture in the colours they were painted) and mixing the
  colours (two named jars in the bowl for each named colour card).
- **Owl Hill Observatory.** A year after the loft is restored the observatory is
  for sale (140,000 coins). Its 15 jobs: the path up the hill, the hilltop grass,
  the copper dome, the lens, the brass telescope, the map-room window, the dusty
  star charts, the door, the sign, the lantern by the door, the stone bench, the
  dome steps, wild thyme on the slope, and two thinking jobs: join the stars and
  the night-sky jigsaw. The evening is its best time: stars over the dome and a
  warm light in the slit.
- **Sunnybank Lido.** A year after the observatory is restored the lido is for
  sale (150,000 coins). Its 15 jobs: the pool tiles, the sun terrace, the
  fountain, the changing huts, the turnstile gate, the sign, the kiosk counter
  and windows, the diving board, the pool steps, palms in pots, litter by the
  pool, deck chairs out (each on its chalk mark), ice creams for the queue (read
  the order board, then scoop the right flavour and number of scoops) and the
  mosaic: a build job whose coloured tiles click in where their colour and shape
  are faintly drawn on the floor of the pool. The pool follows the year: full
  and shining in summer, drained in autumn, empty and rimed in winter, scrubbed
  in spring.
- **Puzzles.** Jobs done by hand with a little thinking, with no tools and no
  robots: put the cups away at the café (each shelf one kind, each coaster one
  glaze), feeding time at the rescue (follow the chart: the right food and the
  right number of scoops for each animal), who sleeps where at the rescue
  (tuck the animals in so every note on the board holds), and at the
  glasshouse water the glasshouse (tap the pipes to turn them until the water
  reaches every pot) and the cutting garden, load the boat at the canal (every
  piece into the hold, tap a piece to turn it; a piece that would leave a gap
  nothing else can fill glides back), and back on the shelves at the bookshop
  (move the end book of a shelf until every series has a shelf of its own).
  The last two also turn up in the seasons at the older places: packing the
  camper van at the cove and books back on the shelves at the cottage, and
  so do the station's: things left behind at the café, whose lead is whose
  at the rescue, parcels of books at the bookshop, and what's missing in the
  cottage attic, the café store room, the bookshop stockroom and the rescue
  barn. The newest ones travel too: a sewing box of buttons and an old locked
  trunk in the cottage attic, a collar of beads for the cat at the rescue, a
  locked chest in the bookshop stockroom (its dials have letters: the first
  letters of the clues spell the word) and a locked suitcase at the station,
  and every place has a jigsaw of itself on autumn and winter afternoons. A
  move that is not right just
  glides back; Hint at the bottom shows one right move. Every puzzle comes out
  different each time and a half-done one is kept for later. Each is a job in
  its place and comes back in that place's seasons, at most one a season.
  Joining the stars (from one numbered star to the next until a figure appears)
  lives at the observatory and comes out under the night sky at the cove and the
  canal. The ice creams at the lido work like feeding time, with an order board
  at the kiosk (flavour and number of scoops for each guest in the queue).
  Jobs made by hand (throwing a pot, stringing beads, sorting buttons, painting
  by numbers, mixing colours, stitching a tear) work the same way, with a Hint button and no tools, and a place has at most one
  of those a season too. After
  9 pm the thinking jobs come last in a season's list, never skipped.
- **The Puzzle Room (beta).** Open from the start, first on the Places list:
  every puzzle in the game, to play in any order and as often as you like, and
  where new kinds of jobs are tried out before they move out to the places.
  There are two corkboards, Thinking and Making, and a tab with an arrow turns
  from one to the other. A board holds its nine newest as cards and any older
  ones as little tickets in rows along the top; in the puzzle room the locked
  box has dice for clues (add them up).
- **Music.** Fifteen quiet tunes of the game's own, one per place (a slow waltz
  on a wooden mallet at the pottery, a soft brushed clickety-clack at the
  station, a music box at the bead shop, slow clockwork with a clock ticking
  softly at the curiosity shop, a folk tune over a pony's gentle trot at the
  stables, something light and plucked at the loft, the slowest and deepest of
  them all at the observatory with an owl far away, a sunny vibraphone and
  lapping water at the lido), in a key that follows the season and slower in the
  evening;
  volume in Settings.
- **Cloud save and friends (optional).** Settings > Save in the cloud gives a
  12-character cloud code; on another phone, I have a code picks the game up
  again, and a phone that finds more progress in the cloud carries on from it.
  The friends button opens friends lists: pick a nickname, start a list and
  share its 6-character code, or join a friend's list. Friends see only
  nicknames and which places each has restored, sorted by name, with no
  ranks or scores; the list adds up the places and keepsakes everyone has
  found together.
- **Café extras.** Stock the drinks fridge: every shelf has its own drink and
  every slot a coloured tag, so each bottle goes back in its place. In the
  seasons the café also wants coffee poured for the guests (iced in summer,
  hot chocolate in winter), napkin holders filled, and the tables and chairs
  put out on their chalk marks. A hungry seal turns up at the cove now and
  then.
- **Christmas lights.** Every winter there is a job at each place to hang the
  lights, bulb by bulb; once they are up they shine in the place's picture.
- **Seasons.** After the restoration the year turns. Each season picks a few
  jobs per awake place from a bigger pool (autumn leaves, snow, frost,
  regrowth, plus seasonal planting and building: sunflowers, tulip bulbs, a
  vegetable patch, window boxes, firewood, a jetty, a brick path). Never more
  than one mowing job a season, and last year's picks are pushed to the back.
  The season carries on into the jobs: outdoor jobs get falling snow and a
  cold light in winter, drifting leaves in autumn, petals in spring and a few
  seeds on the air in summer.
- **A season stays a handful.** However many places are restored, a season
  has at most 37 jobs. The cottage and the newest place always have jobs;
  the others take turns, so a place rests now and then (from thirteen places on
  sometimes two seasons running, never more): it shows "All quiet at the cove this autumn", its finished jobs
  can still be played again, and Places puts a small dot by every place
  with jobs waiting. "Jobs are waiting at ..." points to the next place round
  the village after the one you are at, so a season is a walk round the
  places. A place with nothing left to do this season has a little life of its
  own in its picture, just to look at: smoke from the chimney, a duck on the
  canal, a pony looking out over its door, a hare on the hill, a gull on the
  diving board.
- **Decorate.** Sixteen things to buy for the cottage, from a garden gnome to a
  hot-air balloon, and twelve each for the café, the cove, the rescue, the
  glasshouse, the canal, the bookshop, the pottery, the station, the bead
  shop, the curiosity shop, the stables, the loft, the observatory and the lido.
- **Coins.** Jobs pay modestly on purpose, so tools, helpers, decorations and
  new places take a while to save up for. A season's jobs pay a little more
  each year up to a ceiling, and each restored place past the seventh lifts the
  ceiling a little, so the later places stay within reach.
- **Before and after.** The finish card shows a small before-and-after picture
  of the job, and the next-job card shows a little picture of the mess.
- **Pauses and stopping.** After four jobs and at least twelve minutes, a
  quiet card suggests a pause: what this sitting has done, three slow breaths
  (in for four seconds, out for six) if you like, and two equal buttons,
  "Done for now" and "Keep tidying". The finish card has "Done for now" too.
  After 9 pm, stopping is the main (green) button. While the card is up, the
  next job, the progress bar and the shop dots are hidden. Stopping shows one
  line on what comes next time, fades the sound and goes back to the title.
  It never interrupts a job and the pause card can be switched off in Settings.
- **Goodnight.** After 9 pm, "Done for now" ends with the place in the
  evening: its lights go out one by one with a soft falling note, the music
  fades, and the plan for next time and "Sleep well." come up before the
  title. About six seconds, nothing to collect; Settings > Goodnight lights
  switches it off. In the evening the title also offers **Just one job**: it
  starts the next job straight away and its finish card makes "Done for now"
  the main button.
- **Visitors.** Now and then a muddy (or sandy) dog turns up for a bath, the
  dog next door wants a walk, or the cat next door needs brushing.
- **Small touches.** Evening light after 8 pm (glowing windows, stars), name
  your cottage, save or share a picture of it (camera button).
- **Start again.** Settings > Start a new game begins from 0 coins with the
  cottage overgrown again, simple tools and no robots. Settings, the cloud code
  and the friends list stay; a round number keeps the cloud copy of the old
  game from being picked up again.

## Play on iPhone

Open the link in Safari, tap Share, then **Add to Home Screen**. It then runs
full screen and offline. Progress is saved on the phone (localStorage).

Safari and the Home Screen app keep separate saves, and iOS may clear a Safari
tab's save after a week without a visit. Play from the Home Screen icon. To
move a cottage (Safari to Home Screen, or to another phone), use Settings >
Copy save code, then Paste save code on the other side.

A home screen app looks for a new version whenever it comes back to the
front, and reloads the next time it is put away (never in the middle of a
job), so fixes arrive without closing the app.

Works on iOS 15 and later (a small stand-in covers the canvas `roundRect` that
iOS 15 lacks). Held sideways, the game pauses and asks to be turned upright.

## Cloud service

`cloud/` is a small Cloudflare Worker with one KV namespace, at
https://ivy-cottage.rahbek.workers.dev. No accounts:
a random cloud code (sent in the `X-Ivy-Code` header) is the key to a save;
a friends list has its own short code and only shows nicknames and a few
numbers (the game shows places restored and totals, never a ranking).
Uploads happen at most every two minutes while playing and when the app is
put away; half-done jobs stay on the phone. Deploy from `cloud/` with
`wrangler deploy` (an API token for the account in `CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN`).
